1.1 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
---------------------
Higher education institutions and sprawling corporate campuses across India are 
massive consumers of electrical, water, and logistical resources. However, due to 
archaic, decentralized, and unmonitored infrastructure, university campuses 
routinely lose between 30% and 35% of their total utility budget to unmitigated 
electrical anomalies, HVAC inefficiencies, motor burnouts, and uncoordinated waste 
logistics.

EcoEstate India is an autonomous, hybrid edge-computing campus intelligence and 
facility management platform engineered to resolve this systemic operational 
failure. Designed specifically for university estates, EcoEstate unifies 
multi-protocol IoT sensor telemetry, edge computer vision, game-theoretic 
Explainable AI (TreeSHAP XAI), and an interactive 3D spatial digital twin into a 
unified command center. Operating with a zero-cloud-dependent architecture, 
EcoEstate functions uninterrupted on local campus edge hardware via a dedicated 
offline Desktop Client (.exe) and a rich Web Portal.

1.2 THE CRISIS OF INSTITUTIONAL ENERGY WASTAGE
----------------------------------------------
Institutional facilities suffer from three core visibility blind spots:
1. Sub-Threshold Electrical Waste: Unlike industrial factories with dedicated 
   sub-metering on every machine, university campus facilities operate on aggregated 
   feeder meters. Phantom loads, unoptimized lab equipment, and malfunctioning 
   transformers go unnoticed for months.
2. Delayed Fault Identification: Most maintenance relies on physical complaint 
   logging by students or staff. By the time a water pump bearing failure or 
   transformer oil dielectric breakdown is manually reported, extensive physical 
   damage and fire risks have already occurred.
3. Traffic & Logistical Friction: Campus parking spaces experience peak congestion 
   during morning shift hours, resulting in idle vehicle emissions. Concurrently, 
   campus solid waste bins overflow unpredictably, leading to unhygienic environments 
   and inefficient maintenance routes.

1.3 PAIN POINTS OF TRADITIONAL BUILDING MANAGEMENT SYSTEMS (BMS)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Traditional commercial BMS solutions suffer from fundamental design flaws:
* Rule-Based Static Thresholds: Legacy BMS trigger alarms only when a variable crosses 
  a hard limit (e.g., Temp > 75°C). They fail completely at detecting multivariate 
  degradation (e.g., a pump operating at 60°C and 2.5 mm/s vibration—both individually 
  "within range," but collectively indicating imminent mechanical breakdown).
* Black-Box AI Alarms: Modern AI startups deploy proprietary neural networks that 
  simply flag an "Anomaly: Score 0.92." Facility technicians cannot act on uninterpretable 
  numbers without knowing which specific component is failing.
* Cloud Prohibitive Costs & Bandwidth Dependency: Traditional SaaS BMS stream raw 
  sensor packets to overseas cloud servers (AWS/Azure). This incurs massive recurring 
  subscription fees and causes total system blindness during frequent campus internet 
  blackouts.

1.4 THE CONCEPT OF HVAC SHORT-CYCLING & ELECTRICAL DEGRADATION
--------------------------------------------------------------
A major vector of campus power drain is Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning 
(HVAC) short-cycling.
Definition: Short-cycling occurs when a compressor turns ON, operates for only 1 to 5 
minutes, and shuts down abruptly before completing a standard 15-to-20 minute thermal 
stabilization cycle.

Physics & Electrical Impact:
When an AC compressor motor starts, it draws an inrush current (locked rotor current) 
equal to 400% to 700% of its normal Full Load Amperage (FLA):
    I_inrush = (4.0 to 7.0) * I_nominal

Continuous short-cycling multiplies these high-current spikes throughout the day, 
dramatically elevating peak demand utility tariffs, overheating stator windings, and 
destroying mechanical compressor seals. EcoEstate's sub-metered telemetry detects 
these sharp current cycles within milliseconds, flagging dirty air filters or 
faulty thermostat calibrations before motor burnouts occur.

2.1 THE ECOESTATE SOLUTION PARADIGM
-----------------------------------
EcoEstate re-engineers smart campus management through five integrated layers:
1. Multi-Vector Ingestion: Capturing real-time electrical, mechanical, environmental, 
   waste, and optical camera streams across campus.
2. Dual-Channel Edge Gateway: Routing packets over high-speed Ethernet (TCP/UDP) 
   and local Wi-Fi without internet reliance.
3. Edge AI Processing: Local execution of YOLOv8 ONNX computer vision for parking, 
   Z-score anomaly detectors, and multivariate thermal load predictors.
4. Explainable AI (TreeSHAP): Translating complex anomaly tensors into actionable,

  gateways, UI servers) executes locally on standard on-premise hardware (Mini-PC or 
  edge server). If campus WAN disconnects, zero features fail.
* Plain-English XAI Diagnostics: Maintenance staff receive prescriptive instructions:
  "Water Pump P-2: 68% anomaly driven by high vibration (3.8 mm/s) -> Lubricate bearings 
   and inspect impeller alignment."
* Live Frame-by-Frame Optical Ingestion: Camera frames are parsed locally using an 
  optimized ONNX runtime, streaming annotated video with real-time slot vacancy HUD.
* Spatial Situational Awareness: Instead of navigating disjointed 2D spreadsheets,

   - Ideal for continuous monitoring in 24/7 security guardrooms and substation control rooms.
2. Responsive Web Portal (Next.js 15 & React 19):
   - Modern, high-performance web dashboard accessible from authorized devices.
   - Renders the interactive 3D Spatial Campus Twin.
   - Includes role-based access control (Super Admin, Facility Manager, Auditor).

3.1 MULTI-VECTOR FIELD SENSING INFRASTRUCTURE
---------------------------------------------
EcoEstate monitors five distinct physical domains across the campus:

Domain 1: Air Quality & Weather Station
* Monitored Metrics:
  - PM2.5 & PM10 Particulate Matter (µg/m³) via Laser Dust Scattering sensors.
  - CO2 Concentration (ppm) via Non-Dispersive Infrared (NDIR) sensors.
  - Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs, ppb) via Photoionization detectors.
  - Ambient Temperature (°C) and Relative Humidity (%) via digital hygrometers.
* Objective: Environmental health monitoring, ventilation actuation, and thermal 
  load forecasting baseline inputs.

Domain 2: Electrical Substations & Transformers
* Monitored Metrics:
  - Active Power Draw (kW) via 3-phase digital power analyzers.
  - Transformer Oil Temperature (°C) via industrial RTD probes.
  - Total Harmonic Distortion (THD, %) across voltage and current waveforms.
  - Power Factor (PF, range 0.0 to 1.0) and Neutral Bus Imbalance Current (A).
* Objective: Transformer thermal dissipation safety, power factor penalty prevention, 
  and peak-load shaving.

Domain 3: Heavy Water Pumps & Effluent Treatment Plants (ETP)
* Monitored Metrics:
  - Tri-Axial Vibration Amplitude (mm/s) via piezoelectric accelerometers.
  - Motor Casing Surface Temperature (°C).
  - Discharge Flow Rate (L/s) via electromagnetic flow meters.
  - Phase Current Draw (A) via non-invasive Hall-effect current clamps.
  - Acoustic Cavitation Noise (dB) and Manifold Pressure (bar).
* Objective: Predictive mechanical failure detection, bearing wear identification, 
  and pump cavitation suppression.

Domain 4: Smart Campus Solid Waste Logistics
* Monitored Metrics:
  - Container Fill Percentage (0% to 100%) via top-mounted Ultrasonic Transducers (HC-SR04).
  - Fill velocity rate (% per hour).
* Objective: Automated waste collection dispatching before bin overflow occurs.

Domain 5: Campus Parking Lot Optical Stream
* Monitored Metrics:
  - High-definition video stream (1080p, 15–30 FPS) captured via outdoor RTSP IP cameras.
* Objective: Real-time parking slot occupancy mapping and vehicle detection.

CHAPTER 4: DUAL-CHANNEL HARDWARE GATEWAY & NETWORK PROTOCOLS
================================================================================

4.1 GATEWAY ARCHITECTURE & LOW-LATENCY CONSTRAINTS
--------------------------------------------------
To eliminate ingestion bottlenecks, EcoEstate implements a multi-protocol native 
socket gateway daemon (implemented in `socket_listener.py`). Rather than forcing all 
microcontrollers through heavy HTTP handshakes, the gateway segregates traffic into 
two physical transport channels.

4.2 CHANNEL A: HIGH-THROUGHPUT LAN CHANNEL
------------------------------------------
Designed for wired Ethernet and high-frequency telemetry nodes:
1. TCP Socket (Port 5000):
   - Protocol: Raw JSON Stream / Modbus-TCP.
   - Purpose: Heavy telemetry streams from sub-metered power analyzers and water pump 
     monitoring stations requiring guaranteed delivery (ACK).
2. UDP Datagrams (Port 5005):
   - Protocol: Low-latency UDP Datagrams & Subnet Broadcasts (255.255.255.255).
   - Latency: Sub-millisecond packet transfer (<0.4 ms local propagation).
   - Purpose: High-frequency telemetry bursts from low-cost ESP32/microcontroller 
     sensor clusters where latency is prioritized over heavy transport overhead.

4.3 CHANNEL B: WI-FI REST TELEMETRY INGESTION
---------------------------------------------
Designed for distributed microcontrollers deployed over campus Wi-Fi access points:
* Endpoint: `POST /api/iot/ingest/`
* Payload Structure: Standardized JSON payload containing Device MAC Address, 
  Wi-Fi RSSI (signal strength), Timestamp, and Telemetry Key-Value pairs.
* Ingestion Handling: Multi-threaded queue that validates schema, ingests parameters 
  into the rolling buffer, and updates the active device registry.

4.4 PACKET VALIDATION, HEARTBEATS & FLATLINE BUFFERING
------------------------------------------------------
Real-world IoT networks face packet drops and sensor degradation. EcoEstate 
incorporates two crucial data integrity mechanisms:
1. Rolling Variance Flatline Buffer:
   When an analog probe freezes, it often continues transmitting a static value. 
   EcoEstate calculates rolling variance over a 20-sample sliding window:
       σ² = (1/N) * Σ (x_i - μ)²
   If σ² < 0.00001 while equipment is nominally running, the system flags a 
   "Frozen Sensor Flatline" rather than accepting false data.
2. Device Heartbeat Watchdog:
   Nodes transmit an automated heartbeat. If no datagram is received within 60 seconds, 
   the UI marks the node as "Offline," preventing misleading operational metrics.

5.1 EDGE-FIRST AI INFERENCE PHILOSOPHY
--------------------------------------
Cloud-based AI models introduce transmission latency, high subscription overheads, 
and vulnerability to internet drops. EcoEstate executes 100% of its inference 
pipeline locally using vectorized, quantized model runtimes.

5.2 REAL-TIME EDGE COMPUTER VISION (YOLOv8 ONNX INFERENCE ENGINE)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Implemented in `parking_onnx_engine.py`:
* Architecture: YOLOv8 (You Only Look Once, Version 8) deep convolutional neural network.
* Weights & Export: Pre-trained on vehicular datasets and fine-tuned on parking slot 
  coordinates (`best.onnx`).
* Optimization: Exported to Open Neural Network Exchange (ONNX) format and executed via 
  `onnxruntime`. ONNX utilizes CPU AVX-512 vectorization, allowing 15–25 FPS real-time 
  inference on a standard multi-core CPU without requiring an expensive dedicated GPU.
* Classes Detected:
  - Class 0: 'empty' (Vacant parking slot).
  - Class 1: 'occupied' (Occupied parking slot).
  - Vehicle types: Car, bus, motorcycle, truck.
* Output Metrics:
  - Total parking capacity, occupied slots, available vacant slots, and occupancy rate (%).
  - Real-time base64-encoded annotated video stream featuring green/red bounding boxes 
    and heads-up display (HUD) statistics.

5.3 EXPLAINABLE AI (TreeSHAP XAI ROOT-CAUSE ENGINE)
---------------------------------------------------
Implemented in `shap_explainer.py`:
* The Challenge: Standard ML anomaly detectors output an opaque score (e.g., 0.94), 
  leaving technicians unable to identify the underlying physical malfunction.
* The Solution: TreeSHAP computes exact Shapley values across calibrated feature 
  distributions.
* Operational Mechanics:
  When an anomaly is flagged, the model computes the local attribution of every feature:
      f(x) - E[f(X)] = Σ φ_i
  - Base Value E[f(X)]: Normal operational probability of failure (e.g., 0.08 for pumps).
  - Attribution φ_i: Positive values push toward a fault; negative values indicate normal operation.
* Prescriptive Diagnostics:
  Attributions are mapped to expert facility engineering rules:
  - If φ_vibration dominates -> "Mechanical Bearing Wear / Impeller Misalignment -> Lubricate bearings."
  - If φ_current dominates -> "Winding Degradation / Phase Imbalance -> Inspect stator resistance."
  - If φ_temp dominates -> "Thermal Overload / Cooling Fan Failure -> Clean radiator fins."

5.4 MULTIVARIATE THERMAL COOLING LOAD FORECASTING MODEL
-------------------------------------------------------
Implemented in `ai_engine.py`:
* Predicts campus peak electrical load by coupling ambient outdoor temperature with 
  building occupancy ratios:
      L_pred = L_base * C_thermal * O_factor
  where:
      C_thermal = 1.0 + max(0.0, (T_ambient - 24.0) * 0.042)
      O_factor  = 0.75 + (0.25 * (Occupancy_current / Occupancy_max))
* The model generates a 24-hour predictive load curve, enabling facilities to implement 
  peak-shaving and solar inverter throttling before tariff penalties trigger.

5.5 STATISTICAL OUTLIER DETECTION (Z-SCORE & IQR)
-------------------------------------------------
For ultra-fast, millisecond-level electrical spike detection, EcoEstate utilizes a 
pure-Python rolling Z-score and Interquartile Range (IQR) filter:
    Z = (x - μ) / σ
    IQR = Q3 - Q1
Points falling beyond Z > 3.0 or outside [Q1 - 1.5*IQR, Q3 + 1.5*IQR] are flagged as 
transient electrical anomalies.

5.6 PREDICTIVE WASTE LOGISTICS
------------------------------
A linear regression countdown model computes the exact minutes remaining until a smart 
campus dustbin overflows based on current fill percentage and hourly accumulation rate:
    T_remaining = ((100.0 - Fill_pct) / Fill_rate_per_hour) * 60 minutes
When T_remaining <= 45 minutes, automated dispatch alerts are routed to sanitation staff.

7.1 GAME-THEORETIC SHAPLEY VALUE FORMULATION
--------------------------------------------
In cooperative game theory, the Shapley value determines the fair allocation of payouts 
among players based on their marginal contributions. EcoEstate adapts this to sensor 
fault attribution:

    φ_i(v) = Σ [ (|S|! * (|N| - |S| - 1)!) / |N|! ] * [ v(S ∪ {i}) - v(S) ]
               S ⊆ N\{i}

Where:
* N is the total set of all sensor features (e.g., vibration, temp, current, noise).
* S is a subset of features excluding feature i.
* v(S) is the anomaly characteristic function evaluating the fault probability using subset S.
* v(S ∪ {i}) - v(S) represents the marginal contribution of adding sensor i to coalition S.
* φ_i is the unique Shapley value representing feature i's true impact on the anomaly alarm.

7.2 MULTIVARIATE THERMAL COOLING LOAD EQUATION
----------------------------------------------
The campus electrical cooling demand is modeled as:

    P_cooling(t) = P_base * [ 1.0 + max(0.0, (T_amb(t) - T_setpoint) * α) ] * [ β + γ * (O(t) / O_max) ]

Where:
* P_base is the baseline cooling electrical power in kW.
* T_amb(t) is outdoor ambient dry-bulb temperature measured by campus weather station.
* T_setpoint is target campus indoor thermal setpoint (calibrated at 24.0°C).
* α is thermal expansion factor per degree (empirically derived as 0.042 / °C).
* O(t) / O_max is real-time occupancy ratio (range 0.0 to 1.5).
* β, γ are base and variable occupancy coefficients (β = 0.75, γ = 0.25).

7.3 ROLLING Z-SCORE ANOMALY EQUATION
------------------------------------
For a high-frequency telemetry stream x = [x_1, x_2, ..., x_t]:

    μ_t = (1 / W) * Σ_{k=0}^{W-1} x_{t-k}
    σ_t = sqrt( (1 / W) * Σ_{k=0}^{W-1} (x_{t-k} - μ_t)² )
    Z_t = (x_t - μ_t) / σ_t

Where:
* W is the sliding window size (calibrated to W = 30 samples).
* If |Z_t| > 3.0 (representing 99.7% normal distribution bounds), an electrical spike is flagged.

7.4 ISOLATION FOREST ANOMALY SCORE
----------------------------------
The anomaly score for time-series instance x across n trees:

    s(x, n) = 2^{ - ( E(h(x)) / c(n) ) }

Where:
* h(x) is the path length to isolate point x in an isolation tree.
* E(h(x)) is the average path length across all isolation trees.
* c(n) is the average path length of unsuccessful searches in a Binary Search Tree (BST):
    c(n) = 2 * (ln(n - 1) + 0.5772156649) - (2 * (n - 1) / n)
* Interpretation:
  - If s -> 1.0, instance x is isolated near root nodes and is an anomaly.
  - If s < 0.5, instance x represents standard operational telemetry.

7.5 OBJECT DETECTION LOSS (YOLOv8 CIoU LOSS)
--------------------------------------------
YOLOv8 optimizes bounding box localization via Complete Intersection over Union (CIoU) loss:

    L_CIoU = 1 - IoU + ( ρ²(b, b_gt) / c² ) + α * v

Where:
* IoU is Intersection over Union between predicted box b and ground-truth box b_gt.
* ρ²(b, b_gt) is the Euclidean distance between box center points.
* c is the diagonal length of the smallest enclosing bounding box.
* v measures the consistency of aspect ratios:
    v = (4 / π²) * ( arctan(w_gt / h_gt) - arctan(w / h) )²
* α is a positive trade-off parameter balancing overlap and aspect ratio.

CHAPTER 9: RISK ANALYSIS & MITIGATION STRATEGIES
================================================================================

9.1 SENSOR DRIFT, DECAY & FLATLINE HANDLING
------------------------------------------
* Risk: Low-cost environmental sensors drift over time or freeze due to hardware faults, 
  bypassing static range thresholds.
* Mitigation: Sliding-window variance checks (σ² < 0.00001 flatline detector) combined 
  with cross-sensor spatial correlation. If one temperature sensor flatlines while an 
  adjacent probe fluctuates, the sensor is flagged for maintenance.
* Risk Reduction: ↓ 85%.

9.2 FALSE ALARM SUPPRESSION VIA THERMODYNAMIC BOUNDARIES
--------------------------------------------------------
* Risk: Weather shifts or temporary load spikes incorrectly flagged as equipment failures.
* Mitigation: Physical rule constraints. Telemetry anomalies are validated against physical 
  thermodynamic bounds (e.g., ambient heat absorption limits) before dispatching alarms.
* Risk Reduction: ↓ 80%.

9.3 COMPLETE NETWORK BLACKOUT & SELF-HEALING IMPUTATION
-------------------------------------------------------
* Risk: Campus Wi-Fi or router power failures cause telemetry loss.
* Mitigation: On-device ring buffers on microcontrollers store up to 5,000 samples locally. 
  Upon network restoration, backlogged data is transmitted with timestamps. The backend 
  uses forward-fill and rolling linear imputation to prevent missing-data crashes.
* Risk Reduction: ↓ 75%.
